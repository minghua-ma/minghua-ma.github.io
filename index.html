    Dr. Minghua Ma

    Dr. Ma is a Senior Researcher at Microsoft M365 Research, where he leads the AIOps research direction. His research focuses on AI for software maintenance and reliability: helping engineers diagnose failures, repair software systems, and keep cloud services reliable after deployment. He has worked in AIOps for more than ten years, beginning with his doctoral research on software reliability. He received his Ph.D. from Tsinghua University in 2021, advised by Prof. Dan Pei in the Netman Group. He is an IEEE Senior Member and CCF Senior Member.

    Research Highlights

    AI-Assisted Debugging and Incident Response

    Helping engineers investigate software failures and helping AI agents recover when a debugging or repair attempt fails. This research includes agent recovery (ASE'26), troubleshooting-guide generation (FSE'26), and studies of how AI assistance affects engineers' investigation decisions.

    Software Repair Beyond Code: Build-bench and EvidenT

    Making software build and run requires repairing dependencies, build settings, and compiler assumptions as well as source code. This research addresses both evaluation and repair: Build-bench evaluates cross-architecture repair on 268 real-world failed packages (TOSEM'26), while EvidenT helps repair agents retain evidence across attempts (ISSTA'26). Dr. Ma co-organizes the ICSE 2027 Build-Bench Challenge based on this work.

    Triangle

    Project lead. AI-assisted incident triage for Azure (ASE'25, FSE'26). Its local triage system has operated in production since mid-2024, with more than 30 major Azure services using Triangle as of October 2026. Highlighted by Azure CTO Mark Russinovich in the Advancing Reliability blog.

    Related work: interpretable triage (ISSRE'24, ICSE-SEIP'26) and a survey of triage in software engineering.

    AIOpsLab

    An open-source framework for evaluating AI agents on realistic cloud failures. It combines running microservices, fault injection, workloads, and telemetry to test agents in operational environments. Used in courses at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore.

    Design principles (SoCC'24), evaluation framework (MLSys'25), and tutorial (FSE'25).
